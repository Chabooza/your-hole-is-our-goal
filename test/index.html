      Tailgate meeting

      07:00 · Borehole MW-01 · Riverside terrace

      You are the driller on a three-person sonic crew. The job: core borehole MW-01 to 60 ft, 10 ft at a time, and leave it cased. Dale, Tess and the rig handle all the pipe. You do the drilling, with two thumb pads, and you watch the rig while you do it.

      PUSH, left thumb

      Slide it down to push the bit into the ground. It stays where you leave it. The gauge beside it shows how hard that is for this ground: keep the needle in the green. Too light is slow. Too hard and the sonic head loses its hum, and soft core gets squashed or shaken apart.

      SPIN, right thumb

      Slide it up to rotate. Some spin speeds you up in gravel, till and shale. Past the red line it twists the core. Clay wants hardly any.

      One 10 ft run

      1. Core the run. Push and spin the rod down 10 ft. Ease off when the ground changes and through cobbles: the line at the bottom of the screen tells you how it is going.
      2. Drive the casing. The crew adds a casing joint, then a PUMP button pops up. Pump on, then push and spin the casing down.
      3. Crew at work. They trip the rods out, shake the core out of the barrel and run back in. Fast forward speeds it up. Stay sharp: when a joint is being made up, a prompt pops up. Draw circles or swipe side to side before the bar runs out, or the pipe goes over and the helpers scatter.
      4. Core table. You walk over to see what you recovered, where core was lost and why.
      5. Throw a Zonnic. Now and then while you core, a red button pops up for 3 seconds. Tap it, then tap again when the shrinking white ring sits on the green band. Hit, and it sticks to the helper's hard hat.

      Then the well. At 60 ft the engineer walks over with a drawing. You pour bentonite and sand by drawing circles, never more than 6 in. up inside the casing, then pull a joint of casing and pour again. Prompts pop up for the casing and for every length of PVC. The tape reads late, so stop early: too much in the casing and the next pull rips the well in half.

Tess, core hand: catches the core when you shake it out, and runs the water truck to the hydrant.

      After every sample you walk over to the core table for a close look at what came up: how much you recovered, where core was lost and why, and how close you were to the sweet spot. Inspect core in the top bar takes you back there any time.

      You run all the iron yourself

      Threads. Ease the head down until it lands, then turn FORWARD slowly to make up. REVERSE unscrews. Something must hold the pipe below (a clamp, or the casing arm), or it just spins with the head. When the rotation pressure jumps, the joint is tight.

      Two clamps. The BOTTOM CLAMP only holds. The TOP CLAMP grips and swings. To break a rod joint: joint between the clamps, bottom clamp closed, top clamp open, BREAK-OUT to MAKE to wind it round, close the top clamp, BREAK-OUT to BREAK. Twice. The head alone will never break a rod joint.

      Head slide. With HEAD SIDE on LEFT the head travels far left to centre. On RIGHT it travels centre to far right. Either way it stops dead on centre of mast.

      Casing arm. The rack on your right holds three joints a row. The arm stands one up beside the mast. Slide the head over it, ease down, thread in, bring it back to centre.

      One 10 ft run, start to finish

      1. Core run. Clamps open, sonic on, tune the frequency, drill the rod down 10 ft. No pump water: it washes the core.
      2. Override the casing. Bottom clamp closed, reverse the head off the rods. Raise the casing arm, slide right, thread into the joint, slide back to centre, lower it over the rods onto the casing, make it up. Open the clamp, pump on, drive the casing to the same depth. Clamp and reverse the head off.
      3. Trip out in 20 ft stands. Thread back onto the rods. With an odd number of rods, take the top single off with the head first: pull one joint up, break it with the clamps, spin it out, set it in the top clamp, unscrew the head and call Dale to grab it. For the rest, raise the rods 2 ft, clamp, unscrew the head, slide it LEFT, bring the winch hook down and call Dale to hook up. Pull 20 ft, break the joint with the clamps, call for the chain wrench, then lay the stand on the sawhorses with the winch.
      4. Barrel. Head back to centre and onto the barrel. Pull it clear, TILT out 45 degrees and switch the sonic on so Tess can catch the core.
      5. Back in. Tilt in, run the barrel down, set it in the clamp, then stands back in with the winch (torque each joint with the top clamp), the odd single with the head, and a new rod on top.

      Then the well. At 60 ft the engineer walks over with an installation drawing. Dale and Tess pour bentonite and sand down the casing and thread the 2 in. PVC together: you pour by drawing circles with the mouse (or hold Space), never more than 6 in. up inside the casing, then pull a joint of casing (Enter) and pour again, answering a prompt for every joint and every length of PVC. The tape reads late, and later the deeper the hole, so stop early and top it up. Too much in the casing packs solid, and the next pull rips the well in half.

      Reading the gauges

      Resonance: the penetration rate jumps, the rig stops shaking and the oscillation pressure dips. Every layer has its own sweet spot.

      Rotation pressure at relief (2,800 psi) means a stall. Back off the feed and let the sonic work.

      Pump pressure climbing on an override in clay means a plug. Cut the flow and keep the sonic on, or the hose will blow.

      Water in the casing below the 12 ft water table while the shoe is in sand means sand is heaving in.

      Safety: never move a control while someone has hands on the pipe. Centre the levers and switch the sonic off before you call them in. And never open a clamp that is the only thing holding the rods: they will drop down the hole.
